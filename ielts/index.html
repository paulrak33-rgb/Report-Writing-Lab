Task 1 · Model 1Illustrative Band 6.0

Cinema attendance by age group

IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

The bar chart shows the percentage of people in four age groups who visited a cinema at least once a month in a country in 2010 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words.
Monthly cinema visits by age group (%)
Model response164 words

The bar chart compares the percentages of people in four age groups who went to the cinema at least once a month in 2010 and 2020.

Overall, younger people were more likely to visit the cinema than older people in both years. The figures increased for most age groups, although there was a small fall among people aged 15 to 24.

In 2010, the highest percentage was for the 15–24 age group, at 70%. This was followed by people aged 25–39, with 55%. The figure for those aged 40–59 was lower, at 35%, while only 15% of people aged 60 and over visited the cinema monthly.

By 2020, the percentage for the youngest group had decreased slightly to 65%. However, the rate for 25–39-year-olds rose to 60%, which was close to the figure for the youngest people. There were also increases among the two older groups. The proportion of 40–59-year-olds reached 45%, and the figure for people aged 60 and above doubled to 30%.

Why this level? Clear overview and accurate comparisons, but relatively straightforward vocabulary and sentence structures. These are teaching examples, not officially assessed IELTS scripts.

Task 1 · Model 2Illustrative Band 6.5

Electricity production over time

IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

The line graph shows the amount of electricity generated from solar, wind and coal sources in a country between 2010 and 2020, measured in terawatt-hours (TWh). Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words.
Electricity generation (TWh)
Model response178 words

The line graph illustrates changes in electricity generation from solar, wind and coal in a country between 2010 and 2020, with figures given in terawatt-hours.

Overall, electricity produced from wind and solar increased throughout the period, whereas coal generation declined considerably. Coal remained the largest source in 2020, although the gap between coal and wind had become much smaller.

In 2010, coal produced 180 TWh, compared with 40 TWh from wind and just 10 TWh from solar power. Coal output then fell steadily to 150 TWh in 2014 and 120 TWh in 2016. By 2020, it had dropped to 90 TWh, representing a reduction of half its initial level.

By contrast, wind generation rose from 40 TWh in 2010 to 70 TWh in 2014, before reaching 85 TWh in 2020. Solar power experienced a particularly sharp increase, climbing from 10 TWh to 30 TWh during the first four years. It continued to grow and reached 75 TWh at the end of the period. Consequently, the difference between solar and wind production narrowed to only 10 TWh in 2020.

Why this level? Strong overview, well-selected figures and varied comparisons; some expressions remain conventional rather than especially precise. These are teaching examples, not officially assessed IELTS scripts.

Task 1 · Model 3Illustrative Band 7.0

Household spending in two countries

IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

The two pie charts show how households in Country A and Country B distributed their monthly spending across five categories in 2025. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words.
Monthly household spending, 2025 (%)

Country A: Housing 40%, food 20%, transport 15%, leisure 15%, other 10%. Country B: Housing 30%, food 25%, transport 20%, leisure 10%, other 15%.

Model response187 words

The pie charts compare the proportions of monthly household expenditure allocated to five categories in Country A and Country B in 2025.

Overall, housing accounted for the largest share of spending in both countries. However, households in Country A devoted a substantially greater proportion of their budgets to accommodation, while those in Country B spent relatively more on food and transport.

In Country A, housing represented 40% of total monthly expenditure, twice the share allocated to food (20%). Transport and leisure each accounted for 15%, leaving the remaining 10% for other purchases. Together, housing and food therefore made up three-fifths of household budgets.

The distribution was more balanced in Country B. Although housing was still the largest category, at 30%, its share was ten percentage points lower than in Country A. Food accounted for 25%, five points more than in Country A, while transport also took a larger proportion, at 20%. By comparison, leisure expenditure was slightly lower in Country B, at 10%. Spending on other items was 15%, compared with 10% in Country A. These differences suggest distinct spending priorities, particularly in relation to housing costs.

Why this level? A clear, synthesised overview, flexible comparative language and logically grouped data with precise proportions. These are teaching examples, not officially assessed IELTS scripts.

Task 2 · Model 1Illustrative Band 6.0

Online shopping: advantages and disadvantages

IELTS Academic Writing Task 2

More people now buy products online rather than in traditional shops. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this development?

Write at least 250 words.
Model response253 words

Online shopping has become increasingly popular because people can buy many products without visiting a shop. This change has both benefits and drawbacks for customers and local businesses. This essay will examine the main advantages and disadvantages of shopping online.

One important advantage is convenience. Customers can order clothes, books and household items at any time, even when shops are closed. This is especially useful for people who work long hours or live far from large shopping centres. It can also help parents who have young children and find it difficult to travel to busy shopping areas. Another benefit is that websites allow customers to compare prices easily. For example, someone who wants to buy a new phone can check several stores before choosing the cheapest suitable option.

However, online shopping also creates problems. Firstly, customers cannot always check the quality or size of a product before paying. As a result, they may receive something different from what they expected and have to return it. Secondly, small local shops may lose customers to large online companies. If these businesses close, people could have fewer places to shop and fewer job opportunities in their area. Delivery services also create extra packaging waste, which can be harmful to the environment.

In conclusion, shopping online saves time and can help customers find better prices, but it may lead to disappointing purchases and difficulties for local businesses. In my view, it is useful to shop online for some products, while continuing to support physical shops when possible.

Why this level? Clear four-paragraph structure and relevant examples. Uses the accessible “This essay will examine…” scaffold, with some repetition and limited complexity. These are teaching examples, not officially assessed IELTS scripts.

Task 2 · Model 2Illustrative Band 6.5

Physical inactivity: causes and effects

IELTS Academic Writing Task 2

In many countries, people are becoming less physically active in their daily lives. What are the main causes of this trend, and what effects can it have?

Write at least 250 words.
Model response267 words

In many countries, physical activity is becoming a smaller part of everyday life. This change is linked to modern working habits and the increasing use of technology, and it can have serious effects on both individuals and society.

One major cause is the amount of time people spend sitting at work or studying. Many employees use computers for most of the day and then travel home by car or public transport. Consequently, they have fewer opportunities to walk or move around. Another factor is the popularity of digital entertainment. Instead of playing sports or going outside, some people prefer to spend their free time watching videos or playing online games. These activities can be enjoyable, but they often involve very little movement. In some neighbourhoods, a shortage of safe footpaths or affordable sports facilities makes the situation worse.

The most obvious consequence is poorer physical health. People who exercise less may be more likely to gain weight and develop health problems over time. A lack of activity can also affect mental well-being, since regular exercise often helps people manage stress and improve their mood. In addition, if large numbers of people become less healthy, healthcare services may face greater pressure and higher costs. For instance, clinics may need to provide more support for patients with conditions linked to long periods of inactivity.

In conclusion, sedentary jobs and screen-based leisure are two important reasons for declining physical activity. The effects can include physical and mental health problems as well as increased demands on healthcare systems. Encouraging active travel and providing accessible sports facilities could help address this trend.

Why this level? Clear cause–effect development, relevant consequences and varied linking; ideas could be developed with slightly greater specificity. These are teaching examples, not officially assessed IELTS scripts.

Task 2 · Model 3Illustrative Band 7.0

Public parks: government spending

IELTS Academic Writing Task 2

Some people believe governments should spend more money on public parks and green spaces, even when other public services need funding. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Write at least 250 words.
Model response265 words

Governments face difficult decisions when allocating limited public funds, and investment in parks is sometimes viewed as less urgent than spending on healthcare or education. Nevertheless, I largely agree that public green spaces deserve greater financial support, provided that essential services are not neglected.

The strongest argument for investing in parks is their contribution to public health. Accessible green areas encourage walking, running and informal sports, particularly among residents who cannot afford private fitness facilities. They can also provide a welcome break from traffic and crowded urban environments. For example, a well-maintained neighbourhood park gives families a safe place to exercise and socialise without paying an entrance fee. Over time, these benefits may reduce some of the pressure on local health services.

Public parks also improve the quality of urban life. Trees provide shade, absorb some air pollutants and help make densely populated areas more pleasant. Furthermore, shared green spaces allow people from different backgrounds to meet, which can strengthen community relationships. These advantages are particularly valuable in neighbourhoods where residents have little access to private gardens.

However, increased spending on parks should not come at the expense of urgent medical care or school facilities. A sensible approach would be to protect essential budgets while allocating a modest, consistent share of local funding to maintaining and improving existing green spaces. This may be more effective than building expensive new parks that cannot be maintained.

In conclusion, I support greater investment in public parks because of their health, environmental and social benefits. However, this investment should be carefully planned and balanced against other essential public needs.

Why this level? Sustained, nuanced position, well-developed examples, flexible cohesion and controlled complex sentences. These are teaching examples, not officially assessed IELTS scripts.

Compare Band 6 and Band 7 writing

Band 6-style: “Parks are good because people can do exercise there. This helps them to be healthy.”

Band 7-style: “Accessible public parks encourage regular exercise, particularly among residents who cannot afford private fitness facilities.”

What improves? The second version identifies a specific group, develops the benefit and uses more precise language. Longer sentences alone do not guarantee a higher band.
